# Trying it without setting anything up

Everything else in this manual assumes you have chosen a folder and added a
model key. Neither is much work, but both are decisions, and you should not
have to make them to find out whether this is for you.

So there is a demo. On the start screen, **Try a demo knowledge base**.

## What the demo is

A real knowledge base — three linked notes, a paper, and the paper's index —
that lives in the tab instead of on your disk. Nothing is written anywhere, and
closing the tab throws it away. You will see a **Demo** mark next to its name
for as long as you are in it.

It is the real app, not a video of it. You can read, search, edit, look at the
graph, run the health check, and click the citations — that last one is the
thing worth doing first. In the note about chain-of-thought prompting, click a
blue citation chip: the PDF opens at the exact paragraph the claim came from,
highlighted. Nothing was uploaded to make that happen.

The demo works in browsers that cannot open a local folder at all, because it
never needs one. If you arrived on a phone, or on Firefox or Safari, this is
the part you can still see. See `getting-started` for which browsers can do the
rest.

## Asking it something

The assistant needs a model, and in the demo we lend you one — a small shared
allowance, enough for a few real questions about what is in front of you.

It is deliberately modest, and it is shared with everyone else trying the app
that day, so it can run out. When it does the chat area says so. That is not a
limit on the app: add your own key in `models` and there is no cap at all,
because you are paying your provider directly at their prices. See `keys` for
what that costs and how it is kept.

The allowance is anonymous. There is no sign-up, and nothing identifies you —
the app keeps a random number in your browser so it can tell one visitor from
another for counting purposes, which you can clear like anything else it
stores. See `storage-and-privacy`.

Two things stay off during the trial, because they are not ours to lend: your
own provider account, and anything in the paid tier (see `tools`).

## Keeping what you make

If you like it, you do not have to start again. In the folder menu at the top
left, **Save to a folder…** copies the whole demo — notes, paper, index and any
changes you made — into a folder you choose, and carries on there. From that
point it is an ordinary knowledge base on your disk, and the citations still
work.

Pick an empty folder. The app will refuse one that already has files in it,
rather than mix the demo into something of yours.
